About this map

What is this?

A 2026 field test of the three-actor model first described in Podder et al. (2022). That paper diagnosed a structural funding gap: the built environment accounts for ~21% of global GHG emissions but receives only ~4% of climate-tech venture capital.

This map asks: four years later, which New England states are filling that gap — and how?

The three-actor model

The original paper identified three actors needed to move a contech-for-cleantech startup across the Valley of Death:

①State validatesGovernment funding de-risks early-stage startups
②Philanthropy acceleratesPhilanthropic capital bridges to commercial readiness
③Industry acquiresConstruction/materials incumbents acquire or partner

What changed since 2022?

Federal DOE funding — the backstop behind the original model — has been sharply curtailed since January 2025. 356 DOE awards totaling $12.5B were terminated nationally. In Massachusetts alone, $466M in grants were rescinded. State-level institutions are now the primary actors, not federal backup.

Scoring

Each state is scored on two roles (philanthropy is universally absent across New England and excluded from the composite): State validates (0–2) + Industry acquires (0–2) = composite 0–4. Click any state for the full three-actor breakdown.
